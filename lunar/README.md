## 1. Executive idea

A-01 LUNA explores a modular lunar outpost built around a simple separation of functions:

1. A compact, transportable, pressure-retaining habitat module provides the crew's protected living volume.
2. A separate, replaceable external protection system reduces exposure to the lunar environment.
3. Robotic equipment handles site preparation, regolith movement, placement of shielding material, inspection, and selected maintenance tasks.
4. Life support, power, thermal control, communications, fire safety, and operations are designed as distinct but coordinated systems, with local safe modes and human oversight.

The first research target is deliberately narrow: **a robotically deployed and serviceable regolith-protection system around a standard habitat module**. The project should not attempt to solve an entire lunar settlement in one proposal.

Working concept title: **A-01 LUNA — Regolith-Sheltered Modular Habitat**.

## 2. Why this is a separate branch of A-01

The Earth-based A-01 concept explores human-centred living spaces, modular components, replaceable exterior cassettes, engineering service paths, digital operations, and resilient resource systems. These are useful design principles, but the Earth architecture must not be copied directly to the Moon.

A large transparent dome, extensive glazing, open multi-storey atriums, and ordinary building lifts are not assumed to be suitable lunar solutions. They may impose substantial mass, sealing, thermal, radiation, dust-control, and maintenance burdens.

A-01 LUNA therefore preserves the **modular and serviceable systems philosophy**, while allowing the lunar structure, layout, materials, and operating procedures to be redesigned from first principles.

## 3. Proposed architecture

The concept separates four functional layers:

| Layer | Role | Research questions |
|---|---|---|
| Primary habitat module | Pressure-retaining crew volume and essential internal services | Transport envelope, interfaces, leak detection, repair, redundancy |
| Environmental boundary | Thermal, dust, vacuum, and local contamination control | Seals, penetrations, thermal bridges, dust exclusion |
| Replaceable external protection | Regolith cover, berm, shields, or manufactured local-material elements | Required geometry and mass, placement accuracy, stability, inspection and replacement |
| Interior operations layer | Habitability, displays, lighting, wayfinding, local software and crew tools | Human factors, reduced cognitive load, manual fallback, maintainability |

The external protection layer must not silently become the pressure boundary. Damage or replacement of an external shield element should not automatically compromise the crew's sealed habitat.

### 3.1 Candidate deployment sequence

1. Survey the selected site and verify slopes, surface bearing conditions, hazards, illumination, communications, and access.
2. Prepare a landing and work area while managing plume and dust hazards.
3. Deliver and position a standard habitat module using a mission-compatible cargo system.
4. Verify the habitat interfaces, pressure boundary, power, thermal control, and life-support connections before crew occupancy.
5. Deploy robotic equipment to excavate, move, grade, or place local regolith around designated protection zones.
6. Inspect the protection geometry and the habitat interface; verify that loads and clearances remain within qualified limits.
7. Commission the habitat and its independent safety systems. Keep unverified functions isolated until test evidence is available.
8. Maintain the external protection with replaceable segments and robotic inspection where practical.

This sequence is a planning hypothesis. Landing interfaces, robot capabilities, material properties, structural loads, and commissioning procedures remain open engineering tasks.

### 3.2 Local materials

Potential approaches to investigate include:
- controlled placement of loose regolith as a protective berm or cover;
- mechanically contained regolith-filled elements;
- locally manufactured blocks, panels, or sintered elements, if process energy, equipment, feedstock, and quality control can be justified.

These are alternatives for trade studies, not selected or validated construction methods. A local-material process must be assessed for energy demand, dust generation, contamination, mechanical properties, repeatability, repair, and equipment wear.

## 4. What may transfer from the Earth A-01 project

The following ideas are candidates for adaptation, not proof of lunar readiness:

- **Layered system architecture:** keep primary structure, environmental boundary, replaceable protection, and interior experience distinct.
- **Component passports:** give each maintainable component an ID, parent system, material record, location, lifecycle state, maintenance plan, and evidence status.
- **Serviceable modules:** design external elements to be inspected and replaced without opening the crew habitat.
- **Separated engineering systems:** treat power, water, atmosphere, thermal control, communications, fire safety, robotics, and operations as coordinated but independently protectable systems.
- **Digital operations support:** use software to display system state, maintenance tasks, routes, and procedures; preserve local operation and manual recovery.
- **Human-centred design:** protect privacy, sleep, work, recreation, accessibility, crew autonomy, and psychological well-being.

The current Earth digital twin is a software concept with uncalibrated scene units. Its visual dimensions and animations must not be used as lunar engineering values.

## 5. Lunar constraints to address

NASA describes the lunar South Pole as a candidate region for sustained exploration and highlights the need for surface infrastructure, power, robotics, dust mitigation, and other technologies. A-01 LUNA must explicitly study at least the following constraints:

### Radiation and micrometeoroids
Evaluate shielding strategies and habitat placement using mission-specific radiation and impact environments. No shielding thickness or dose reduction is claimed in this concept brief.

### Abrasive lunar dust
Study dust ingress at interfaces, seals, connectors, moving joints, radiators, sensors, and equipment. Include cleaning and inspection methods that do not create unacceptable crew exposure or wear.

### Thermal extremes and long dark periods
Develop a site- and mission-specific thermal model. The power and thermal architecture must account for the lunar illumination cycle, storage, survival loads, heat rejection, and periods of reduced activity. Do not assume continuous sunlight or a simple day/night schedule at every candidate location.

### Vacuum and pressure boundary
The habitat pressure vessel, hatches, penetrations, windows if any, and docking interfaces need independent qualification. The external regolith protection is not a substitute for a certified pressure vessel.

### Low gravity and construction operations
Assess excavation, transport, material placement, anchoring, dust trajectories, equipment traction, and stability under lunar gravity. Earth construction machinery cannot be assumed to work unchanged.

### Limited mass, volume, energy, and crew time
Quantify landed mass, packaged volume, deployment time, power, consumables, spares, crew time, and robot duty cycle. Minimise unnecessary complexity and Earth resupply.

### Autonomy and communications
Essential control and safety functions must continue locally during communication loss. Remote supervision may help, but the concept must not depend on continuous Earth communication or unrestricted human intervention.

Primary NASA reference: [Lunar Surface Technology](https://www.nasa.gov/lunar-surface-technology/).

## 6. First engineering demonstrator

The initial demonstrator should be one full-scale or appropriately scaled **habitat-to-shield interface**, not a complete base.

A useful test programme would include:

1. A representative sealed habitat wall/interface specimen.
2. A replaceable external shield element and its retention/inspection features.
3. A small robotic handling or placement demonstration.
4. Dust ingress and contamination testing on representative joints and connectors.
5. Thermal-vacuum and mechanical tests planned with qualified laboratories and appropriate standards.
6. Failure cases: misalignment, jammed mechanism, loss of power, sensor failure, damaged shield element, and interrupted communications.
7. A documented manual or robotic recovery path that does not require opening the habitat.

The test article's material, geometry, mass, forces, temperatures, and acceptance criteria must be defined by qualified engineering work. No performance is claimed before tests.

## 7. Evidence and engineering gates

Each value in the project should be labelled as one of: **unknown, assumed, proposed, calculated, reviewed, tested, approved, or as-built**.

Before claiming feasibility, the project needs:
- a selected mission scenario, crew size, duration, and site assumptions;
- mass and packaged-volume budgets;
- structural load paths and geotechnical assumptions;
- regolith properties and handling test evidence;
- radiation and micrometeoroid protection analysis;
- thermal-vacuum analysis and heat-rejection strategy;
- power generation, storage, distribution, and survival-load estimates;
- life-support interfaces and resource budgets;
- dust-control, fire-safety, emergency, and maintenance plans;
- robotic operations and failure-recovery analysis;
- human-factors and crew-habitability review;
- independent review by qualified specialists.

An animation, rendering, spreadsheet estimate, or digital twin is not a qualification test or safety certification.

## A-01 AIR-01 — Autonomous Atmospheric Infrastructure

A-01 AIR-01 is the proposed atmosphere and life-support subsystem for the lunar habitat. It separates the external regolith-processing factory from the clean habitat ECLSS, combines lunar-resource oxygen research with water electrolysis and air revitalization, and documents the initial crew mass balance, reserve scenarios, power-budget method, and failure-mode map.

- [AIR-01 engineering concept brief (English)](air-01/README.md)
- [Концепция AIR-01 на русском](air-01/README_RU.md)
- [AIR-01 工程概念说明（简体中文）](air-01/README_ZH.md)
- [Traceable mass-balance data (JSON)](air-01/mass-balance.json)
- [System block diagram (Mermaid source)](air-01/system-block-diagram.mmd)

The preliminary four-person reference case uses 0.84 kg O2 and 1.0 kg CO2 per crewmember-day from a NASA life-support study. The arithmetic highlights that hydrogen from water electrolysis alone is insufficient to process all metabolic CO2 through an ideal Sabatier loop. MRE results are treated as a ground-demonstration reference, not a lunar operating rating. Total system power, mass, atmosphere composition, storage sizing, and qualification remain open engineering tasks.
