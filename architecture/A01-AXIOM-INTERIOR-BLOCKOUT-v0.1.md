# A-01 Interior Blockout — Axiom-inspired spatial study v0.1

**Date:** 21 November 2026  
**Related prototype:** [A-01 v3.8.0 Interior Blockout](../digital-twin/eva-sphere-a01-v380-axiom-inspired-interior-blockout.html)  
**Status:** concept study; not dimensioned design.

## Objective

Create a first spatial composition for the A-01 interior that can be reviewed in 3D before detailed engineering. The Axiom passenger ship in WALL‑E is a visual and organizational reference only: a tall shared void, layered public circulation, readable functional districts and a visible relationship between transport, housing and shared amenities.

The design must remain original to A-01. No copyrighted Pixar geometry, textures, models or extracted game assets are included.

## Spatial framework

The v0.1 blockout uses five schematic levels:

| Level | Primary function | Spatial idea |
|---|---|---|
| L00 | Arrival, assistance, logistics, sanitary and technical support | Clear entry sequence and central public floor |
| L01 | Community, retail, food and meeting | Active public ring around the atrium |
| L02 | Residential and shared lounges | Repeated perimeter modules with quiet internal galleries |
| L03 | Family care, wellness, rest and garden | Calm green pockets, family-support rooms and restorative spaces |
| L04 | Observation, library, work and dispatch | Upper ring with views into the shared volume |

The central atrium remains visually open across levels. Ring galleries organize circulation and provide views into the atrium. A limited number of bridges connect the inner gallery to the perimeter route. These are massing devices only; bridge placement, widths and supports remain unverified.

## Circulation and service logic

- Provide a continuous accessible route; lifts are the assumed primary accessible vertical connection.
- Treat escalators and stairs as supplementary, never as replacements for an accessible route.
- Keep service logistics and maintenance access distinguishable from public and residential circulation.
- Route technical risers and service distribution in coordinated zones that can be reached for inspection and replacement.
- Locate sanitary and family-support spaces according to demand, privacy, acoustic separation and accessible travel distances, not simply by visual symmetry.
- Preserve direct, legible connections from every public zone to protected egress routes after a separate fire strategy is developed.

## Design questions for the next iteration

1. Confirm the actual dome inner radius, usable floor plates, structural grid and floor-to-floor heights.
2. Decide whether the atrium is fully open or partially bridged, and model sightlines from accessible standing and seated positions.
3. Establish realistic occupant loads and peak arrival/egress flows before fixing public-zone capacity.
4. Place toilets, showers, family rooms, rest areas, food service and waste collection using travel-distance and plumbing-stack coordination.
5. Separate public, resident, staff, service, restricted and emergency routes.
6. Coordinate lift count, lift traffic, stairs, smoke compartments, pressure zones and accessible evacuation with qualified specialists.
7. Add actual room boundaries, doors, furniture clearances, vertical shafts and plant spaces only after a dimensioned brief exists.
8. Connect the geometry to an IFC/BIM data model when the architectural grid and object properties stabilize.

## Validation gates

- **Geometry:** calibrated metre scale, dimensioned plans and sections, clash review.
- **Accessibility:** turning circles, clear widths, gradients, reach ranges, accessible toilets and user testing.
- **Fire/life safety:** smoke strategy, compartment boundaries, protected stairs/lobbies, evacuation scenarios and code review.
- **Building services:** HVAC airflow and pressure, water demand, sanitary drainage, electrical distribution, maintenance access.
- **Structure:** load paths, robustness, vibration, foundations and geotechnical inputs.
- **Operations:** cleaning, deliveries, waste handling, equipment replacement, maintenance routes and staffing.

## Status and limitations

The v3.8.0 HTML is a visual 3D massing blockout using Three.js. All dimensions are arbitrary scene units. Its lift animation is decorative; no system is connected to physical equipment. It is not a BIM model, engineering calculation, code-compliance assessment, evacuation design or construction document.
