# A-01 X — Sensory Bridge: first-person perception and sensor fusion

**Document:** A01X-SENSE-01 · **Revision:** 0.1 · **Date:** 22 November 2026  
**Status:** engineering architecture proposal; not a connected runtime

## 1. Objective

Give Android-01 a first-person observation channel so a permitted AI observer can receive what the agent's head camera sees, together with time-aligned physical and environmental measurements.

There are two distinct use cases:

1. **Digital Android-01:** an eye camera rendered from the humanoid head pose inside the Three.js digital twin.
2. **Physical robot:** calibrated cameras and hardware sensors, preferably exposed through ROS 2 standard message types.

The AI should receive observations, not pretend to possess human subjective experience. A camera image is visual input; an IMU is a measurement of acceleration and rotation; motor current is an actuator-load indicator. None of these alone proves pain, fatigue, stress or emotion.

## 3. Sensor model: separate observation from interpretation

Every incoming value must be labelled as one of:
- measured: directly reported by a sensor with calibration metadata;
- simulated: generated by the digital twin;
- derived: calculated from other observations;
- inferred: model-produced interpretation, never presented as a raw sensor fact.

Every reading should include source ID and sensor type; acquisition timestamp; clock-synchronization status; value and SI unit; coordinate frame where spatially relevant; quality and calibration state; freshness and validity; provenance; and software / firmware version.

Missing, stale, uncalibrated or contradictory data must remain visibly unknown. Do not fill gaps with guessed values.

## 4. Sensory channels

### 4.1 Vision — agent's eyes

**Digital twin**
- Attach a dedicated AgentEyeCamera to the Android-01 head transform, not to the visitor's orbit camera.
- Define camera intrinsics (resolution, horizontal / vertical FOV and focal parameters) and extrinsics relative to the head frame.
- Keep the robot's eye direction independent of the spectator's UI camera.
- Render the eye view to a separate capture target so the agent can look around while the operator sees an orbit or map view.
- Publish RGB frames and, when available, synchronized depth, object detections and camera pose.
- Mark synthetic geometry and synthetic people as simulation data.

**Physical robot**
- Use calibrated RGB or RGB-D cameras; add a stereo pair only when depth and baseline calibration are available.
- Preserve camera calibration and optical-frame metadata. An image without calibration can support visual description but cannot reliably support metric geometry.
- Do not assume one camera sees behind the head, inside opaque objects or around corners.

For the browser simulator, a rendered canvas can be captured as a real-time media track using HTMLCanvasElement.captureStream(). The eye stream must be a separate canvas / render target, not an accidental screen recording of the operator's desktop.

### 4.2 Audio and spatial sound

- Microphone array or simulated binaural / spatial audio input, with timestamp and sample format.
- Optional direction-of-arrival estimate only when microphone geometry and calibration support it.
- Separate environmental sound-level metrics from speech recognition.
- Raw audio capture is off by default; enable only for a visible, time-bounded session with explicit permission.
- Never record bystanders' speech for routine acoustic comfort monitoring. Prefer calibrated sound-pressure metrics and event labels without speech content.

### 4.3 Vestibular and balance proxy

- IMU: 3-axis linear acceleration, angular velocity and orientation quaternion, including covariance / status.
- Head and torso orientation relative to gravity.
- Estimated tilt, angular rate, step / gait phase and balance margin as derived values.
- In the digital twin, derive these from avatar pose and simulated motion, but label them simulated.

An IMU does not directly measure a human vestibular sensation. It provides motion measurements from which limited motion features can be calculated.

### 4.4 Proprioception and touch

For a physical Android-01:
- joint position, velocity and effort / torque where the hardware exposes them;
- actuator current, voltage, temperature and fault state;
- foot contact and pressure distribution;
- wrist / hand force-torque and tactile arrays where fitted;
- bumper, proximity and distance sensors.

For the digital Android-01:
- joint transforms and articulation state;
- collision contacts from the physics engine;
- commanded vs achieved pose and navigation progress;
- simulated actuator load / energy only if a stated model produces it.

Do not call an animation pose or guessed force a measured tactile sensation.

### 4.5 Environmental context

The perception packet may include measurements from the agent and from the zone's fixed building sensors:
- air temperature (°C), relative humidity (%RH), barometric pressure (Pa);
- CO₂ (ppm), PM2.5 / PM10 (µg/m³), CO (ppm) and VOC indicators only with specified sensor and calibration;
- illuminance (lux), sound-pressure metrics (dB with weighting and integration time);
- surface / radiant temperature if a suitable sensor exists;
- vibration (m/s² or a declared spectral metric);
- air speed (m/s) if measured with suitable instrumentation.

A robot-mounted sensor is not automatically representative of a whole room. Associate it with current zone, position, height and time. For comfort decisions, combine mobile observations with fixed sensors and building-system airflow / occupancy context.

### 4.6 Navigation and body state

Include estimated pose in a named coordinate frame and floor / zone ID; localization confidence and last localization time; current route, next waypoint, obstacle distance and clearance; lift / door state only when received from a trusted simulator or approved equipment interface; battery state, motor temperature and controller health.

For a human-centred proxy, use separate synthetic profile attributes. Do not infer an actual person's fatigue, health, disability or emotional state from the robot's movement.

## 5. Transport and AI connection

Recommended split:
- **Local real-time control:** sensor acquisition, collision checks, balance and emergency stop remain on the robot / local simulation. They must continue without cloud AI.
- **Media transport:** WebRTC for interactive audio and real-time media where both endpoints support it.
- **Structured telemetry:** a versioned data channel or authenticated local WebSocket / ROS 2 bridge for timestamped sensor packets.
- **Visual reasoning:** send selected eye frames as image inputs to a vision-capable model. Do not assume an audio-only Live frontend accepts a continuous video track. Current OpenAI documentation distinguishes the GPT-Live audio frontend from image delegation to a vision-capable backend; current Realtime models also document image inputs in user-message content.
- **AI response:** return concise scene observations with frame timestamp, detected objects, uncertainty and recommended next inspection step.
- **Command path:** keep perception and actuation separate. The vision model cannot issue arbitrary joint commands. Any allowed high-level action passes through a whitelist, local motion planner, limits, collision monitor, operator stop and independent safety supervisor.

Keep API keys on a trusted server. The browser receives only short-lived session credentials. Never expose a long-lived model or robot-control secret in HTML or JavaScript.

## 6. Practical update rates

Initial values are engineering targets for prototyping, not universal requirements:
- head RGB view: 5–10 frames/s locally; send a lower-rate sampled stream (for example 1–2 frames/s) to a cloud vision model for scene understanding, with event-triggered bursts;
- depth / obstacle map: 5–15 Hz depending on sensor and local planner needs;
- IMU: typically 50–200 Hz to a local control stack; summarize / downsample for high-level AI;
- joint state / actuator telemetry: 20–100 Hz locally where hardware supports it; send summaries and anomalies to AI;
- environmental comfort sensors: often 0.1–2 Hz depending on sensor response time; do not oversample slow sensors;
- audio: only in a user-enabled session, at a rate negotiated by the audio transport.

The fast local control loop must not wait for an AI response. Sampling and compression must be bounded to protect network and GPU resources.

## 7. Observation → interpretation → response

Example sequence:
1. Android-01 reaches a waypoint and stops.
2. The eye camera captures a frame at time T; IMU, pose, joint state and nearest-obstacle readings are aligned to T.
3. Sensor gateway validates timestamps, frames, calibration, freshness and quality.
4. The vision model describes only what is visible: “door leaf appears closed; handle is on the right; floor marker partly obscured.”
5. Sensor fusion adds measured facts: “localization confidence 0.91, obstacle range 0.74 m, right wrist effort elevated.” These values are illustrative only and must come from actual sources in runtime.
6. AI suggests an inspection or navigation target, but does not directly drive the joints.
7. The local planner checks traversability and safety; operator or approved policy authorizes the next step.
8. A post-action observation verifies whether the result occurred.

The AI should be able to say “I cannot tell from this frame” or “sensor data is stale.” That is a required success behaviour, not a failure.

## 8. Safety, consent and privacy

- Separate robot-camera consent from any human camera or microphone permission.
- Clear recording / streaming indicator, on/off control and emergency stop.
- No covert camera or microphone activation.
- No face recognition, emotion recognition or voice identification by default.
- Process locally where possible; minimize frame rate, resolution, retention and uploaded fields.
- Do not send raw audio or video to external providers unless the operator explicitly enables that session and the data route is disclosed.
- Encrypt transport; authenticate endpoints; bind local services to loopback by default; use mutual authentication for remote robot connections.
- Use per-session, short-lived credentials and replay protection.
- Keep logs of who initiated a session, sensor sources, model version, commands, approvals and shutdown.
- Protect firmware, OTA updates and the local network. Never bridge the AI data channel into safety PLCs or life-safety circuits.
- On loss of link, stale frames, sensor disagreement, localization loss or overheating: stop or enter a prevalidated local safe state. Do not continue blind.

## 9. ROS 2 mapping for a future physical robot

Prefer standard message types where they fit:
- sensor_msgs/msg/Image or CompressedImage plus CameraInfo;
- sensor_msgs/msg/Imu;
- sensor_msgs/msg/JointState;
- sensor_msgs/msg/BatteryState;
- sensor_msgs/msg/Temperature, RelativeHumidity, FluidPressure and Illuminance;
- sensor_msgs/msg/LaserScan or PointCloud2;
- geometry_msgs/msg/WrenchStamped, PoseStamped and TwistStamped;
- tf2 transforms for head, camera, base and tool frames;
- diagnostic_msgs for source health.

Do not invent topic names, frame IDs or driver capabilities before the target hardware is selected. Map actual vendor topics to this neutral observation envelope.

## 10. Test plan

**S0 — interface discovery:** obtain the exact v0.3.5 source/runtime or a published URL and verify window.A01Agent, window.EVA_SPHERE, head pose and renderer API. Do not guess method signatures.

**S1 — simulated eye:** add a separate head-mounted camera and verify camera pose while the operator orbit camera remains unchanged.

**S2 — deterministic packet:** emit synthetic frame metadata, pose, IMU-like state and zone measurements with explicit simulated provenance. Verify timestamps, units and stale-data flags.

**S3 — transport:** test local authenticated frame and telemetry relay; verify disconnection, reconnect, backpressure and manual stop.

**S4 — perception:** send a small set of sampled frames to a vision-capable backend; compare object / doorway descriptions with known scene ground truth and record uncertainty.

**S5 — physical adapters:** only after hardware and safety review, bridge calibrated ROS 2 sensors. Keep actuation disabled during perception-only tests.

**S6 — supervised walkthrough:** run fixed routes with a human operator watching the safety view; test doors, lifts, narrow passages, glare, low light, sound sources, sensor dropouts and network loss.

## 11. Acceptance criteria

The sensory bridge is not accepted until:
- eye camera pose matches the Android head transform within a documented tolerance;
- spectator camera and agent eye camera are independent;
- every measurement carries timestamp, unit, source, provenance and quality;
- simulated and physical values are visually distinct;
- image and telemetry timestamps stay aligned within the declared limit;
- stale, missing and contradictory data are surfaced, not silently replaced;
- no camera / microphone starts without explicit permission;
- AI cannot bypass local collision, motion, emergency-stop or equipment-safety logic;
- link loss produces the tested safe state;
- logs can reconstruct the observation and decision sequence.

## 12. Explicit limitations at this revision

No live Android-01 is published at the inspected public endpoint; no eye stream or sensory packet was received in this session. No physical person, robot camera, microphone or sensors were accessed. This document defines the adapter contract and test path; it does not claim a working robot connection.
