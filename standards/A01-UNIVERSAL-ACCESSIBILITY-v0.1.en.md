# A-01 — Universal Accessibility & Family Comfort Standard v0.1

**ID:** A01-ACC-STD-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Release date:** 2026-11-21  
**Status:** conceptual design requirements; specialist review required  
**Preliminary code jurisdiction:** Russian Federation  
**Scope:** site access, entrances, horizontal and vertical circulation, public and residential spaces, toilets, showers, family care, wayfinding, rest and evacuation.

> This is an internal A-01 design standard. It is not a national standard, statutory code, construction document, expert approval or certificate of compliance. Applicable regulations and editions must be confirmed for the actual building use, classification, occupancy and site.

## 1. Purpose and core principle

A-01 should be usable with dignity and as independently as practicable by people with different abilities and life circumstances. Accessibility is a property of the whole complex, not a separate “special route” or a single isolated cubicle.

The design considers wheelchair users; people using walkers, canes and other mobility aids; people with vision, hearing, cognitive or sensory disabilities; pregnant people; older adults; people recovering from surgery or temporary injuries; parents and caregivers with infants, children and strollers; and companions or support persons.

Core principles:

1. **A shared route:** people should use the same main entrance, lifts and public routes wherever practicable, without humiliating detours through back-of-house areas.
2. **Independence:** controls, doors, sanitary fixtures and wayfinding should be usable without mandatory staff assistance.
3. **Choice and privacy:** a person may choose independent use, caregiver assistance or a family room without disclosing unnecessary personal information.
4. **Safety:** accessibility must not weaken fire safety, hygienic separation, acoustic privacy or engineering reliability.
5. **Continuity:** an accessible route must connect the site, entrance, lift, destination room and return route; it must not end at a threshold or lift lobby.
6. **Verifiability:** each requirement needs a drawing, measurable parameter, responsible reviewer and verification method.

## 2. User scenarios

Design reviews should cover, at minimum:

- independent use of manual and powered wheelchairs;
- toilet transfer from either the right or left side;
- use of walkers, crutches, canes and handrails;
- navigation with a white cane or guide dog;
- receiving information without sound and without relying on a screen;
- moving with a stroller, more than one child and luggage;
- infant care, feeding and expressing milk in a private setting;
- seated showering, with or without an assistant;
- calling for help from a wheelchair, seat or floor level;
- movement during fatigue, pain, limited stamina or sensory overload.

Review the complete journey between activities, not only the geometry of an individual room.

## 3. Horizontal routes and entrances

### ACC-01 — Continuous step-free route

A continuous accessible route should connect passenger drop-off, accessible parking or transit access to the main entrance, reception, lifts, public spaces, residential modules and sanitary rooms.

Design conditions:

- no unmarked steps, high thresholds, protrusions or unexpected pinch points;
- slip-resistant surfaces with stable performance when wet and in winter conditions;
- drainage that prevents ponding, icing and hazardous grates along routes;
- doors with manageable opening force or automatic operators where required;
- sufficient clear widths and maneuvering areas for turning, passing and waiting;
- weather-protected arrival and entrance areas;
- no furniture, temporary stands, queues or decorative objects inside the required clear route.

Final widths, slopes, heights, opening forces, maneuvering clearances and surface requirements must be selected from the applicable clauses of SP 59.13330.2020 and the actual floor plan. The digital twin does not prove compliance.

### ACC-02 — Doors and access control

- Prefer clear, step-free openings over turnstile barriers; any access-control point must include a fully accessible passage.
- Automatic doors need safe detection zones, anti-trap protection and a defined manual or backup mode.
- Controls must be reachable and distinguishable by touch and sight.
- Facial recognition or a phone app must not be the only way to enter.
- A safe state on power failure must be coordinated with the fire and security strategies.

## 4. Vertical circulation

### ACC-03 — Lifts

Accessible lifts are the primary means of step-free travel between A-01 levels. Their number, location, car dimensions, door openings and capacity must be determined through passenger-flow analysis, accessibility requirements, fire strategy and operational scenarios.

Verify and provide:

- entry and maneuvering space for a wheelchair user with a companion;
- call buttons and in-car controls at accessible heights and with usable actuation;
- tactile labels, contrast, Braille and audio/visual floor and direction information;
- accurate leveling between car and landing;
- sufficient door dwell time and obstruction protection;
- two-way communication with a staffed control point and confirmation that an alarm was received;
- backup power and a defined failure scenario without promising uninterrupted availability;
- capacity for a person, companion, stroller and necessary equipment.

Escalators and moving walkways are supplementary circulation features. They do not replace an accessible lift, are not the only accessible route and must not be counted as an independent evacuation route. An ordinary passenger lift must not be treated as a fire evacuation lift unless a dedicated, approved design provides for that use.

### ACC-04 — Stairs and escalators

Stairs should have continuous handrails, visually distinguishable step edges, adequate lighting and contrasting markings. Escalators must not obstruct accessible routes, turning spaces, door zones or evacuation paths. Clear alternative routes and accessible wayfinding are required.

## 5. Universal toilets and showers

### ACC-05 — Multiple sanitary scenarios

A-01 should provide a combination of:

- accessible cubicles within common toilet blocks;
- separate universal toilet rooms offering privacy;
- combined toilet, shower and washbasin rooms;
- showers usable from a seated position;
- family rooms for infant and child care;
- women’s personal-hygiene rooms where required by the building brief.

Where multiple universal cubicles are provided, layouts should allow transfer to the toilet from both the right and left sides. Users should not have to disclose their preferred transfer side to staff or ask for access to a locked back-of-house room.

### 5.1. Regulatory dimensional references

Clauses 6.3.2–6.3.3 of SP 59.13330.2020 specify more than room geometry. In a building accessible to people with reduced mobility that has visitor toilets, at least one universal toilet room is required; accessible cubicles within toilet blocks are additionally provided at 5% of the total cubicle count. A universal room may also serve as a family toilet for parents with children. An accessible cubicle must provide at least 0.8 m of clear space beside the toilet for a wheelchair, a 1.4 m diameter turning space, and an outward-opening door. Universal rooms require fixed and fold-up support rails. Accessible toilet rooms use a toilet with back support, a 0.45–0.50 m seat height and a 0.70 m length. Table 6.1 lists the following minimum plan dimensions, measured clear, for the applicable room types. Applicability depends on building use and configuration. Actual fixture placement, transfer zones and door swings must be checked on drawings.

| Room type | Minimum clear plan dimensions |
|---|---:|
| Accessible toilet cubicle within a toilet block | 1.65 × 2.20 m |
| Universal toilet room, toilet centered | 2.20 × 2.25 m |
| Universal toilet room, toilet against wall, right- or left-side transfer | 1.70 × 2.20 m |
| Enclosed shower | 1.80 × 1.80 m |
| Combined shower, toilet and washbasin room | 2.30 × 2.40 m |
| Combined sanitary room with shower and changing area | 2.70 × 2.70 m |
| Mother-and-child room (table entry) | 2.50 × 3.00 m |

These are regulatory reference minima, not recommended comfort dimensions for every A-01 room. A-01 should provide additional clear space wherever needed for a wheelchair, companion, safe transfer and child care. Accessible showers require wheelchair approach space, a slip-resistant floor, a threshold-free tray, an outward-opening door, a fold-down seat no higher than 0.48 m above the shower tray, a handheld shower and wall-mounted grab rails; the seat must be at least 0.5 m deep and long. Taps in accessible sanitary rooms should have lever operation and a thermostat; separate hot- and cold-water controls are not used. Table dimensions do not replace a detailed layout and fixture check.

### 5.2. Toilet equipment

- toilet and grab rails arranged for safe transfer;
- fold-up support rail anchored to an appropriately designed substrate;
- washbasin usable from a seated position, with no hazardous exposed trap;
- taps and dispensers operable with one hand;
- mirror and hooks usable while seated or standing;
- slip-resistant floor, waterproofing and cleanable surfaces;
- legible, non-glare signs;
- space for a companion where needed;
- emergency call or two-way communication as required by applicable rules, with an activation device reachable from floor level;
- authorized emergency external door release while preserving everyday privacy.

An alarm must reach a staffed response point, provide confirmation and have an operating response procedure. Call activation should be confirmed to the user (for example visually, audibly and/or by vibration, as applicable). A decorative indicator without a real response process is not an emergency system.

### 5.3. Showers

- step-free entrance without a raised tray;
- slip-resistant surface and engineered drainage;
- fold-down seat with suitable fixing;
- grab rails, handheld shower on an adjustable holder and reachable controls;
- thermostatic mixing to reduce scald risk;
- place for clothes, care products and personal items;
- accessible assistance call;
- privacy and acoustic protection;
- access to inspect and clean the floor drain without dismantling finishes.

The sanitary and drainage design must be coordinated with water supply and wastewater systems. Waterproofing, gradients, odours, ventilation, leak detection and maintenance access require separate verification.

### 5.4. Hygiene, privacy and acoustics

Toilets should allow users to clean the room safely and quickly after accidental soiling. Provide smooth moisture-resistant finishes, practical local cleaning access, suitable floor drains where permitted, few inaccessible joints and correctly designed ventilation.

Acoustic privacy must be checked at partitions, doors, ceiling voids, soil stacks and ventilation ducts. Extract air from sanitary rooms so odours do not migrate to adjacent cubicles, corridors or living areas. Fragrances are not a substitute for removing contaminated air.

## 6. Family care room

### ACC-06 — Family Care Room

A-01 should provide family care rooms on public levels. They are intended for a mother, father, guardian or another caregiver. This is a calm, private room, not merely a changing station and not a replacement for an accessible public toilet.

Base provision:

- safe changing surface with raised sides and a restraint strap;
- clear space for a stroller and wheelchair maneuvering;
- comfortable feeding and recovery chair;
- private area for breastfeeding and expressing milk;
- handwashing basin and child-hygiene provision;
- soap dispenser, paper towels and lidded waste bin;
- small table and storage for personal items;
- protected electrical outlet in a safe location;
- food warmer with instructions and scald-risk controls;
- accessible assistance call;
- clear occupancy indication that does not disclose a user’s identity.

A breast-milk refrigerator may be an option only with temperature monitoring, storage instructions, a sanitation log and clear responsibility rules. Safe storage must not be claimed without an operational procedure.

The room should provide a privacy lock while preserving emergency release and an assistance call. Breastfeeding must not be restricted to this room: it is an additional option, not a mandatory place to feed.

SP 118.13330.2022, clause 5.64, specifies mother-and-child rooms for the building types listed there and a ratio of one place per 1,000 visitors for the specified facilities. This ratio must not be applied automatically to every building type; verify A-01’s actual classification. A-01 includes family care as a user-centred design function, with capacity to be calculated separately.

## 7. Wayfinding, vision, hearing and cognitive access

### ACC-07 — Multichannel wayfinding

Information should be presented in at least two suitable modes: visual, auditory and/or tactile. The combination depends on the location and task.

- clear pictograms and short labels;
- adequate text/background contrast and controlled glare;
- tactile labels and Braille at applicable doors and controls;
- a continuous tactile ground-surface indicator system where required by the access plan;
- audio and visual confirmation for lift calls and important announcements;
- induction systems at service points where user review identifies a need;
- consistent names for floors, areas and routes;
- simple step-by-step route instructions, not only a complex map;
- a way to request help without a smartphone or internet connection.

For tactile ground-surface indicators, consider GOST R 52875-2025, effective 2026-07-01 and replacing GOST R 52875-2018. Materials, textures, locations and interfaces with adjacent surfaces must be coordinated with the overall circulation system, not added as decorative strips.

### ACC-08 — Sensory comfort

Provide a quiet room or calm area with adjustable lighting, low background noise and clear access. It is not a medical room or isolation space. It supports short rest, sensory regulation and recovery.

Audio signals, light effects and digital screens must not create flashing, harsh glare, persistent noise or wayfinding overload. Provide a calm lighting mode and alternative ways to receive information.

## 8. Rest, furniture and operations

### ACC-09 — Rest points

Provide stable seats with backs and armrests along longer routes and near public functions. Location and quantity must account for travel distances, movement flows, visibility, passing space and unobstructed evacuation.

For parents, provide stroller parking and places for short feeding breaks or settling a child. For people with limited stamina, provide places to pause before a route becomes exhausting.

### ACC-10 — Operational accessibility

- Accessible toilets, showers, care rooms, lifts and routes must not become storage areas.
- Carts, décor, queues and cleaning equipment must not temporarily block accessible routes.
- Equipment and controls need regular inspections; faults must be reported promptly to a responsible staff member.
- Cleaning must not leave slippery wet floors or close every accessible room at once.
- Signs and wayfinding must be updated when the building changes.
- Staff must be trained in respectful assistance, wheelchair handling, emergency-call response and support for visitors with different needs.
- Feedback should be voluntary and must not require disclosure of diagnosis or disability status.

## 10. Fire safety and evacuation

### ACC-11 — Coordinated evacuation for people with reduced mobility

The evacuation strategy must be developed jointly by a fire-safety engineer and an accessibility specialist. It must cover people who cannot independently descend stairs quickly, people with vision or hearing disabilities, parents with children and visitors unfamiliar with the building.

Required design tasks:

- define safe routes, fire compartments and permitted movement scenarios;
- provide refuge or other protective arrangements only where supported by calculations and applicable requirements;
- provide two-way communication with staff/control and confirmation that a call was received;
- make critical alarms available in accessible modes;
- define equipment, trained staff, roles, accountability and assistance procedures;
- provide backup power for systems that require it;
- keep waiting areas and evacuation paths clear;
- conduct drills and test scenarios with participants with disabilities.

Ordinary escalators are not evacuation routes. Passenger lifts are not evacuation facilities unless specifically designed and approved for that function. Evacuation chairs or staff assistance do not replace required refuge and route design.

The regulatory basis includes SP 1.13130.2020, “Evacuation routes and exits,” with applicable amendments. The actual strategy depends on A-01’s fire concept, occupancy and height characteristics, and formal review.

## 11. Requirement model and verification

Each A-01 requirement should be recorded with:

- requirement ID (for example, A01-ACC-03-001);
- user scenario and location;
- drawing, section, BIM/IFC object or system-map reference;
- measurable parameter and unit;
- regulatory source and clause, where applicable;
- selected design value and justification;
- evidence state: unknown, assumed, proposed, calculated, reviewed, tested, approved or as-built;
- responsible specialist and reviewer;
- verification method and date;
- user feedback and corrective-action status.

### Mandatory review gates

1. **Layout review:** accessible route from site to every functional space; doors, turning, transfers and clear areas.
2. **Sanitary-room review:** clear-dimension plans, fixtures, grab rails, door swings, alarm, cleaning, ventilation and acoustics.
3. **Family-room review:** stroller, changing table, feeding, privacy, basin, storage and help access.
4. **Wayfinding review:** tests with participants with vision, hearing and cognitive disabilities.
5. **Lift review:** dimensions, controls, waiting, capacity, redundancy and maintenance.
6. **Evacuation review:** approved calculation, communication, staff roles, accessible alarms and practical drill.
7. **User review:** walkthrough with people who use wheelchairs, walkers, canes and strollers; record and resolve identified barriers.
8. **Independent audit:** accessibility, fire safety, sanitary systems and operations specialists before moving to the next design stage.

A 3D visualization, presentation or apparently spacious room does not prove accessibility. Clear-dimension drawings, specifications, calculations, control checks and real user testing are required.

## 14. Open questions

- final building use and classification;
- design number of residents, staff and visitors;
- area and clear dimensions of each level;
- number and configuration of residential modules, public areas and sanitary rooms;
- lift count, traffic analysis and redundancy;
- approved fire strategy and evacuation scenarios;
- final jurisdiction, site and utility conditions;
- participation of a user review group at concept and detailed-design stages.

Until these issues are resolved, requirements remain proposed. v0.1 sets a design direction and review gates; it does not certify regulatory compliance or construction readiness.
