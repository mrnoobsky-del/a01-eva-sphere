# MODNET — animated concept storyboards

**Revision:** v0.1  
**Status:** Concept visualization / discussion material

These animated GIF storyboards illustrate possible logistics sequences for exchangeable modules. They are visual concepts, not engineering drawings, approved layouts, or evidence that a mechanism has been built or tested.

## 1. A-01 arrival and module exchange

![A-01 arrival and exchange storyboard](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/f9ecd4_7cc50237dc2249fc98a78d9c396f57bb~mv2.gif)

The sequence explores a delivery vehicle approaching the A-01 facade, aligning with a receiving bay, transferring a detachable module, and leaving the bay available for another operator. Vehicle and company branding are illustrative placeholders, not confirmed partners.

## 2. MODNET shared dock-slot cycle

![MODNET shared dock-slot cycle](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/f9ecd4_ce277d2788434dfb9e25313da8894964~mv2.gif)

The sequence illustrates the shared-slot business idea: one operator arrives with a module, completes the exchange, and another module can later use the same dock slot. The storyboard is a simplified concept and does not define the exact lifting or transfer equipment.

## 3. D-03 cargo vestibule / transfer bay

![D-03 cargo vestibule transfer sequence](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/f9ecd4_07202b7db3f04347b192754098ff3067~mv2.gif)

This is the clearest visual reference for a dedicated cargo transfer bay. The truck approaches an enclosed transfer opening; the module is aligned, lifted or supported by a transfer platform, moved through the bay, and the carrier departs.

**Interpretation:** this should be treated as a separate logistics vestibule (cargo airlock) with an outer vehicle approach and an inner controlled transfer boundary. It is not an ordinary pedestrian entrance. The storyboard does not yet establish whether the module physically enters the occupied building envelope or is transferred into a dedicated service zone.

## 4. Operating principles to carry into engineering

1. No occupants inside a module during road transport, lifting, or transfer.
2. Keep the vehicle approach / exchange apron, cargo vestibule, and internal dock as separately defined zones.
3. Do not allow the outer and inner barriers to be open simultaneously unless a verified engineering safety case explicitly permits it.
4. Verify module identity, mass, centre of gravity, lifting points, support condition, clearances, and slot compatibility before transfer.
5. Establish positive mechanical locking and independent secondary retention before connecting utilities or accepting the module for operation.
6. On removal, isolate and disconnect utilities, confirm the module is in transport state, transfer it to the carrier, and inspect the vacant dock before releasing the slot to the next operator.

## 5. Important limitations

The images do not define real dimensions, structural capacity, lifting loads, door forces, fire resistance, ventilation, pressure relief, drainage, electrical ratings, or vehicle turning geometry. These must be calculated and verified for the selected site, module, carrier, and applicable jurisdiction.

The image labels (including A-01, MODNET, D-03 and company marks) are illustrative. No vendor, carrier, customer, or commercial partner is confirmed by these visuals.
