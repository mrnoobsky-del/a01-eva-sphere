# A-01 Olfactory Environment — design brief

**Document:** A01-OLF-01 · **Revision:** 0.1 · **Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Status:** research concept and candidate design policy; not construction or medical guidance.

A-01 treats scent as part of the multisensory built environment, but its default is **no intentionally added fragrance in shared spaces**. Clean air does not need perfume.

## What we learned from Wonderscent

Wonderscent is a fragrance-discovery platform, not a building scent-diffusion system. A November 24, 2026 industry profile describes mood-led search, personalized fragrance recommendations, color/aura cards, scent notes, longevity and community-derived mood tags across a catalogue of more than 50,000 fragrances from 3,000 brands.

A-01 adopts the mood-first discovery pattern, not its commercial catalogue or brand identity. Our output may be lighting, acoustics, materials, plants, food-source management or no scent at all. Link: https://www.beautymatter.com/articles/wonderscent-raises-2-25-million-pre-seed-funding

## Core policy

- **O0 — NO_ADDED_SCENT:** default for care, recovery, entry, atrium, corridors, lifts, housing circulation, control rooms and technical areas.
- **O1 — NATURAL_AMBIENCE_ONLY:** natural garden or food aromas only; no artificial diffuser. Cooking odours require local source capture and ventilation coordination.
- **O2 — PERSONAL_NEAR_FIELD_SAMPLE:** voluntary non-aerosol sample on a blotter, only in a clearly labelled optional Olfactory Studio.
- **O3 — ISOLATED_RESEARCH_SESSION:** future concept requiring separate ventilation, product safety review, fire/electrical review and commissioning. Not approved by this brief.
- **X — PROHIBITED:** shared-area diffusion, scent injection into common HVAC, fragrance masking of faults, scented cleaning in common areas.

Accessibility Standards Canada published a draft CAN-ASC-2.11 annex in September 2026 with scent-free recommendations for shared spaces, fragrance-free/low-VOC maintenance products, signage, accommodation processes and contractor practices. The document is a draft, not a final regulation: https://accessible.canada.ca/standards-and-technical-guides/standards-and-technical-guides-database/can-asc-211-accessible-ready-modular-and-prefabricated-housing/15-annex-b-design-multiple-chemical-sensitivities-informative

The US Access Board recommends fragrance-free policies in public/commercial buildings, including restrictions on fragrance-emitting devices, sprays, potpourri, incense and scented candles: https://www.access-board.gov/research/building/indoor-environmental-quality/general-recommendations/

The American Lung Association notes that both natural and synthetic fragrance sources can release pollutants and that fragrance exposure may cause symptoms in some people: https://www.lung.org/clean-air/indoor-air/indoor-air-pollutants/fragrances

## Safety boundary

The prototype does not control diffusers, HVAC, sensors or physical equipment. A VOC/TVOC sensor is not an "electronic nose" that can identify a particular perfume. Fragrance preferences are optional user data; no health or emotional diagnosis is inferred. An isolated Olfactory Studio starts with personal, non-aerosol samples. Any airborne diffusion would require a separate design and approval.

This release is concept documentation only. It is not a ventilation design, a chemical safety assessment, a perfume formulation or permission to install equipment.
